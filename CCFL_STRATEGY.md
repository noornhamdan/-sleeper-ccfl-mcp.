# CCFL assessment protocol

1. Start every assessment with a successful live refresh; state freshness and use current settings/rosters. Never reconstruct the roster from earlier conversation conclusions.
2. Pull recent opportunity trends and baseline lineup comparisons. Distinguish provider forecasts, observed statistics, imported analyst adjustments and missing evidence.
3. For close decisions, verify current practice/availability, routes and targets per route where available, high-value touches, quarterback/line circumstances, coverage/pass-rush matchups, market lines/props and weather. A snap count is not a route count. Cite dated sources and reconcile conflicts.
4. Run active/inactive and sourced reduced-workload scenarios. List the exact evidence that changes each recommendation. Treat sub-one-point expected differences as close calls, not precise edges.
5. Build a legal lineup with late-game flexibility and explicit kickoff verification. Optimize expected points by default; do not chase volatile players solely because the opponent is unbeaten. Change a variance strategy only with defensible distribution evidence.
6. Evaluate additions against the expendable roster spot and current free-agent pool. Separate immediate lineup gain, multi-week coverage, injury insurance and durable dynasty upside. Do not sacrifice a promising prospect just to hold three defenses. Do not revive abandoned trade debates. Respect the user's excluded targets.
7. Preserve the pregame recommendation, sources, assumptions and reversal triggers. Review projected opportunity and prediction error against a baseline across weeks, not just who happened to score touchdowns.

Every refresh should communicate: recommended lineup; confidence; close alternatives; what changed since the last verified snapshot; reversal triggers; roster improvements with their opportunity cost; and important evidence gaps. Software output is one input to the recommendation, not automatic permission to change Sleeper.
